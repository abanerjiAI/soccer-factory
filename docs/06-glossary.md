# Glossary

A-Z terms used in the app. Each has a short definition and a soccer example. If you present a term, be ready to explain it in your own words.


## A

- **Access loss** - Demand that goes unserved because nobody can arrive within the time budget (a distance problem, not a workload problem). Soccer: a counter-attack down the right where no defender can get across in time.
- **Accessibility** - How easily a place can be reached from where agents are. Soccer: a corner flag is less accessible than the penalty spot.
- **AGV** - Automated guided vehicle: a self-driving cart that moves materials. The factory version of a player.
- **Assumed parameter** - A number the model needs but that was chosen, not measured. Soccer: a top speed of your choice.

## B

- **Baseline** - The result before any disturbance, used for comparison. Soccer: how we defend with 11 players.
- **Bottleneck** - The step or agent that limits the whole system's output. Soccer: the one midfielder every attack runs through.

## C

- **Capacity** - How much work an agent or station can handle per period or at once. Soccer: how many attackers one defender can mark.
- **Constraint** - Whatever sets the limit on performance. Finding it is the second-most useful IE skill: look where work waits.
- **Cycle time** - The time to complete one repeat of a task. Soccer: how long one pressing sequence takes from trigger to recovery.

## D

- **Dead zone** - A region nobody can reach within the time budget. Soccer: the space behind a high line.
- **Demand field** - A map of where requests or events arise, and how often. Soccer: where the ball tends to land.
- **Disturbance** - A change that hits the system mid-run. Soccer: a red card, or a pitch part becoming unusable.

## E

- **Effect size** - How big a difference is, not only whether one exists. Soccer: 'we won 2-0' versus 'we won 2-1 on one shot'.
- **Experiment log** - The record of saved runs with their settings, so you can repeat them.
- **Expert parameters** - The extra settings in The Lab beyond the three main sliders. Change them with care and note every change.

## H

- **Hard coverage** - A place counts fully covered or not at all, depending on the time budget. Soccer: a yes/no 'can reach in 2 s'.
- **Hash** - A short code computed from a run's inputs and results. Same hash means the same run. Used when you reproduce a run.
- **Homography** - A mapping that turns a camera image of a flat surface into true flat coordinates. Measure It uses it to turn a photo into pitch positions.
- **Hypothesis** - A prediction you can test and that can be wrong. Soccer: 'a higher line reduces dead zones'.

## I

- **Importance (weighting)** - Counting some places more than others. Soccer: the box counts more than the touchline.

## K

- **Kinematics** - The study of motion: position, speed, acceleration, ignoring forces. Soccer: sprint speed and acceleration limits.

## L

- **Layout** - How stations or agents are arranged in space. Soccer: a formation. Factory: linear, U-shaped or functional.
- **Little's law** - For a stable system in the long run: average number in the system = average arrival rate x average time in the system. Soccer: if a player is stopped on average 3 times a minute and each takes 2 seconds, he holds on average 0.1 duels (3/60 x 2) at a time. Only valid on long-run averages in a stable system.
- **Load** - The amount of work assigned to an agent. Soccer: how many requests one player is responsible for.

## M

- **MISMATCH** - The message when a reproduced run's hash differs from the saved one. Settings, seed or code changed.
- **Monte Carlo** - Repeating a simulation with random inputs to see the spread. Soccer: replay the same match 500 times with random noise.

## O

- **Operator** - A person who runs a task or machine. The factory version of a player.

## P

- **p-median** - A location problem: place p facilities to minimise average distance to demand points. Soccer: where to put p defenders.
- **Pareto front** - The set of options where you cannot improve one metric without worsening another. Soccer: more pressing cover versus more fatigue.
- **Precedence** - A required order of tasks. Factory: cut before weld. Soccer has no such fixed order.

## Q

- **Queue** - Items or people waiting for service. Soccer: attackers waiting for one defender to be free.

## R

- **Ratio** - One number divided by another. Soccer: shots per possession.
- **Recovery** - How fast and how far a metric returns after a disturbance.
- **Resilience** - How well a system holds up under, and bounces back from, a shock.
- **Response time budget** - The time allowed to reach a point. Beyond it, the point counts as not reached. Soccer: 2 seconds to close down.

## S

- **Sensitivity** - How much a result changes when an input changes. Soccer: how much coverage changes if top speed drops by 10%.
- **Simulation** - Running a model of a system step by step on a computer. Here it is SIMULATED, not measured.
- **Soft coverage** - Coverage that fades with time instead of a yes/no cutoff. Soccer: arriving late still partly helps.

## T

- **Throughput** - How much work finishes per unit time. Soccer: shots created per ten minutes.
- **Top speed** - The highest speed an agent can reach. Soccer: your sprint speed.

## U

- **Uncertainty** - How much a number could be off. Measure It shows it, as every real measurement has error.
- **Utilisation** - Share of available time or capacity actually used. Soccer: 70% means busy 70% of the time. Near 100% means no slack, and queues form.

## W

- **Work order** - A request to do a job. Factory: active job. Soccer: the ball needing someone to reach it.
- **Work sampling** - Estimating how time is used by taking many quick random or regular looks. Soccer: ticking every minute whether a player is running, walking or standing.
