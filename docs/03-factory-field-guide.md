# Factory field guide

Use this when you visit a plant, or look at a process at school, a hospital or a warehouse. The goal is to see the same five ideas in a real place. You are an observer, not an inspector, and nothing you measure is a verdict on anyone.

## Before you go

- Ask permission and say what you are doing. Offer to share what you learn.
- Bring a clipboard, a stopwatch (phone is fine if allowed), pencil, and a printed observation sheet (below).
- Decide one **process** to watch: e.g. one order picked in a warehouse, one patient through check-in, one tray through a school lunch line.

## What to observe, what to ask

| Your Five | Look for | Ask |
|-----------|----------|-----|
| Reach | How far people or items must travel; what can be reached in the time allowed | "What is the target time for this task, and what happens when it is missed?" |
| Importance | Which stations or items matter most | "Which step would hurt most if it stopped?" |
| Constraint | Piles of waiting work; someone always busy | "Where does work wait? What is the slowest step?" |
| Shock | What happens when someone is out or a machine stops | "What did you do the last time this broke?" |
| Transfer | What is the same as soccer, and what is not | "What rules or safety limits stop you from moving things the shortest way?" |

## Five mini-measurements

### 1. Travel (spaghetti) diagram - Reach
- Sketch the floor. Follow one worker or item for 10 minutes. Draw a line for each movement.
- Count steps or estimate metres, if allowed to pace it.
- *Look for:* crossing lines, long returns, backtracking.

### 2. Work sampling - Constraint (utilisation)
- Every minute (use a repeating timer), glance at each person and tick one: **working / waiting / walking / other**.
- Take at least 30 observations per person, more if you can.
- Utilisation estimate = working ticks / total ticks. Small samples are rough; say so.

### 3. Cycle-time study - Constraint
- Time the same task 10 times from start to finish. Record each time.
- Report the average, the fastest and the slowest. A wide gap means variation worth asking about.
- **Cycle time** here means time for one repeat of the task. Ask permission before timing named individuals; time the task, not the person.

### 4. Queue count - Constraint / Shock
- At a chosen point, count how many items or people are waiting, once a minute for 10-15 minutes.
- Note when it jumps. If you can, note arrivals and departures too.
- *Look for:* what causes spikes (a rush, a break, a breakdown).

### 5. Layout sketch - Importance / Transfer
- Draw stations as boxes, mark arrows for flow of work.
- Circle the critical station and mark which pairs of stations interact most.
- Compare to the three layouts in **Factory Layouts**: is this linear, U-shaped, or functional/scattered?

## Etiquette and safety

- Follow the host's safety rules first: protective gear, marked walkways, no entering restricted areas.
- Stay out of the way. Never stand in forklift or AGV paths.
- Do not photograph people, screens or documents without permission.
- Record tasks, not names. Do not share what you saw publicly without asking.
- Do not offer to "fix" anything. Observe, thank them, and ask what they would like improved.
- Be honest about limits: a stopwatch study of one hour is a snapshot, not proof.
