# Present it

You may show this to a club audience or to an admissions officer. What you need is a clear story, honest limits, and the ability to explain every number you show.

## The 90-second answer

Fill the blanks only with what you ran and what you believe.

> "I play soccer, and I wanted to learn what Industrial Engineering is. IE is about making systems work better by measuring them. I built a lab that treats a soccer team like a system. **Reach:** in my simulation, the team covered [your number] of the pitch within [your time budget]. **Importance:** when I weighted the important areas, it changed to [your number], because [your reason]. **Constraint:** the bottleneck was [who/where], with load [your number]. **Shock:** after a red card, we dropped to [your number] and recovered in [your number]. **Transfer:** the same engine on a factory floor showed [what you saw], but it breaks down at [your limit]. These are simulated under assumed settings, and I can explain how each number is computed."

## NAIC showcase booth

**On screen:** **Start Here** as the idle screen so people can drag a player. Keep a second tab open on **What-If** and one on **Factory Layouts**. Keep **My Notebook** ready, not the Coach.

**Have ready:** a printed one-page summary of Your Five, your field-guide observation sheet if you did a visit, and a printed pitch for the **Measure It** page if you plan to demo it (it needs a camera and https or localhost).

**Three questions to expect:**

| Question | Honest answer pattern |
|----------|----------------------|
| "Is this real data?" | "The simulation uses assumed parameters. [If you loaded data: I compared them to real tracking speeds on Real Match Data and found [your finding]. Otherwise: I have not validated it on real match data.]" |
| "How much did AI write?" | "AI helped me [what it actually did: code, explanations, tutoring]. I can explain every metric and re-derive it, and I can show the Study Guide and Checks." |
| "What would you do next?" | "Measure a real process with the field guide, then compare it to the model, and see where my assumptions fail." |

Other likely ones: "Why soccer?" (it is a system I understand well, so I could focus on the maths) and "What did you find that surprised you?" (answer only from your own Notebook).

## Talking about AI assistance

- Say plainly what AI did and what you did. If asked, disclose it. Do not hide it.
- The **Coach** only explains numbers the app gives it. It does not calculate results.
- You should be able to explain, in your own words and at a whiteboard, the main metrics: coverage, weighted coverage, load/utilisation, and baseline-to-recovery.
- **Build Notes** has the metric definitions and an honest status. Mention that it exists.
- If you do not know something, say "I don't know yet, here is how I would find out".

## What NOT to claim

- Do not say the results are measured or real. They are **simulated under assumed parameters**.
- Do not say a soccer team *is* a factory. The analogy is methodological.
- Do not say a hypothesis was proven. Verdicts are "supported" or "not supported" in this model only.
- Do not quote any number you cannot trace back to a run in your **Experiment Log**.
- Do not say the model would improve a real plant or a real team.
- Do not say or imply anything about how this affects admissions.
- Do not claim to have studied a real plant unless you did, and then describe exactly what you timed or counted.
